First trip to China

What to know before you go.

China can feel surprisingly easy once the few unfamiliar systems are set up. This guide focuses on the things first-time visitors are most likely to need before landing: mobile data, payments, apps, trains, hotels, food, language, and a realistic route.

Last reviewed: September 16, 2026

Quick answer

Do these before your flight: confirm your current entry requirements with an official source, arrange mobile data, set up at least one mobile payment option, download your core China apps, save every hotel address in Chinese, and keep a backup payment method. Then keep your first itinerary simple enough that you are not changing cities every day.

1. Check entry rules before you book around them

China entry and visa rules can change by nationality, passport type, route, and travel date. Before buying non-refundable transport around a visa assumption, check the latest information from the Chinese embassy or consulate that serves your location, your airline, and other relevant official authorities.

  • Make sure the name on flights and train bookings matches your passport.
  • Keep a digital and offline copy of your passport information page and key bookings.
  • Save your first hotel name, phone number, and address in both English and Chinese.
Do not rely on an old blog post for visa-free or transit rules. Treat entry policy as time-sensitive.

2. Get your phone working before you need it

Mobile data affects almost everything else: maps, ride-hailing, translation, payment verification, train bookings, and hotel communication. Sort it before you land if possible.

Data

Compare international roaming, a travel eSIM, or a local SIM. Check whether your phone is unlocked and whether the plan works in mainland China.

Offline backup

Screenshot hotel addresses, flight details, train numbers, and emergency contacts. Do not assume every app will work exactly as it does at home.

3. Set up payments before arrival

QR payments are extremely common in mainland China. Many foreign visitors prepare Alipay or WeChat Pay using a supported international card, but verification, card support, transaction limits, and merchant acceptance can vary.

  • Try account verification and card linking before your flight.
  • Keep a physical bank card as a backup.
  • Carry a modest amount of cash for edge cases rather than depending on one payment method.
  • If a QR payment fails, try a smaller amount or a different supported method instead of assuming your whole account is unusable.

4. Download a small set of useful apps

You do not need twenty apps. Start with the few that solve the biggest first-trip problems.

Alipay

Payments and many mini-services.

WeChat

Messaging, local communication, and payment features where available.

Amap

Local maps, addresses, and public transport information.

Didi

Ride-hailing in many cities.

Trip.com

Useful for travel bookings and English-language trip management.

5. Keep trains and city transport simple

High-speed rail is one of the easiest ways to connect major Chinese cities, but first-time visitors should leave extra time for large stations and identity checks.

  • Use the exact passport details required by your booking platform.
  • Check the station name carefully; large cities often have multiple major railway stations.
  • Arrive earlier than you would for a small commuter station, especially on your first trip.
  • Save the Chinese name of your destination so you can show staff or a driver.

6. Save your hotel details in Chinese

A hotel address in Chinese is one of the highest-value screenshots on your phone. It helps with taxis, ride-hailing pickups, late-night returns, and asking a local person for directions.

  • Keep the hotel name, Chinese address, and phone number together.
  • Carry your passport when the hotel requires it for check-in or registration.
  • If you are arriving very late, message the property in advance rather than assuming the front desk procedure is the same everywhere.

7. A few phrases matter more than perfect Chinese

You do not need to speak Mandarin to enjoy a first trip, but short, specific phrases can remove friction at restaurants, hotels, taxis, and pharmacies.

  • Save phrases for allergies or foods you cannot eat.
  • Keep your destination written in Chinese instead of relying only on pronunciation.
  • Use translation tools, but confirm serious allergy, medical, legal, and safety information directly with a qualified person.

8. Do not overbuild your first itinerary

China is large. A first trip feels much better when you spend enough time in each place to understand it instead of collecting train stations.

5–7 days

Choose one region or two major bases. Example: Beijing + Xi'an, or Shanghai + a nearby destination.

8–10 days

Three bases can work well. A classic first-time route could combine Beijing, Xi'an, and Shanghai.

11–14 days

Add a slower nature or culture stop rather than adding several one-night cities.

The right route depends on your arrival city, departure city, pace, interests, and tolerance for long travel days. A personalised plan is more useful than copying a generic city list.

First-trip checklist

  1. Confirm current entry requirements from official sources.
  2. Arrange mobile data and save offline backups.
  3. Set up at least one mobile payment method and keep a backup.
  4. Download your essential apps before arrival.
  5. Save every hotel address in Chinese.
  6. Check railway station names carefully.
  7. Keep your route simple enough to enjoy the trip.
