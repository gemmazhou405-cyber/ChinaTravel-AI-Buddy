### Quick answer（id="quick-answer"）

> For three full days in Nanjing:
>
> - **Day 1:** the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum and Ming Xiaoling, in the Zhongshan scenic area.
> - **Day 2:** Nanjing Museum or the Presidential Palace, then the Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall. It's a solemn historical site, so give it time.
> - **Day 3:** the city wall, the Confucius Temple area and the Qinhuai River.
>
> Watch out for Mondays: the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, Nanjing Museum and the Memorial Hall all close on Mondays, with holiday exceptions. The mausoleum is free but needs an online booking, and Ming Xiaoling is ¥70. Book the museum and each Memorial Hall exhibition area separately, with your passport, and check whether your train uses Nanjing or Nanjing South station.
>
> [Tickets, hours and Monday closures](#tickets)

### H2：Day 1: the Zhongshan scenic area（id="day-1-the-zhongshan-scenic-area"）

> 1. Take Metro Line 2 to Muxuyuan station, Exit 1. Start with the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum: it's free but needs an online booking in your passport name, it's open 8:30–17:00, and it's closed on Mondays except national holidays. The tomb chamber itself isn't open to visitors.
> 2. Be ready for stairs and a long outdoor walk. If mobility or weather makes the climb unsuitable, spend more time in an accessible part of the scenic area instead.
> 3. Add one substantial second stop. Ming Xiaoling (¥70) is about 500 m from Exit 1. The ¥100 combined ticket also covers Linggu, the Music Stage and Meiling Palace, but don't try to see them all in one relaxed day.
> 4. Ming Xiaoling is part of the UNESCO-listed Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties. Read a little of its history before you go, and take your time on the tree-lined approach.
> 5. Head back into the city for dinner. A full Qinhuai evening on top of two big eastern sites is too much for one relaxed day.

### H2：Day 3: walls, southern lanes and Qinhuai（id="day-3-walls-southern-lanes-and-qinhuai"）

> 1. Explore one accessible section of Nanjing's city wall or Zhonghua Gate after checking the specific entry point and ticket rules. The walls are long; choose a segment rather than planning a complete circuit.
> 2. Walk through the southern old city toward the Confucius Temple area (free to walk, though some sights charge; Metro Line 3, Fuzimiao station). Look into the side streets and local food rather than staying on the busiest shopping frontage.
> 3. Walk by the Qinhuai River in daylight or early evening. The painted-boat trip boards near the Confucius Temple: ¥80 by day (9:00–18:30) or ¥100 at night (18:30–22:00).
> 4. If you have a late train, keep this day flexible, collect your luggage and allow time for security at your exact station.

### H2：Where to stay and how to arrive（id="where-to-stay-and-how-to-arrive"）

> - Stay near a useful metro interchange in the central city if you want balanced access to Zhongshan, the museums and Qinhuai. A hotel in the Qinhuai area suits evening walks but means a longer trip east.
> - Nanjing and Nanjing South are different railway stations. Check the station printed on your ticket; Shanghai also has several departure stations.
> - Book on 12306 or Trip.com, with your details exactly as in your passport. Tickets go on sale 15 days before departure, and China Railway's FAQ confirms foreign passengers can use their passports for real-name tickets. [How to book China trains](/china-train-travel-guide/)
> - If you're flying in, check the airport-to-city metro or road route and your hotel's late check-in. Save the hotel's Chinese name and address offline.
> - Before you book a hotel, check its "Guests Accepted" line on Trip.com ([how](/china-hotels-for-foreigners/#check-before-booking)), and check the nearest metro station and a realistic travel time to the sights.
>

> | Site or journey | Check before travel | Common mistake |

> | Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum | An online booking in your passport name; not on a Monday | Assuming you can walk in, or that the whole scenic area uses one ticket |

> - Summer heat, rain and holiday crowds make outdoor walking much slower. Start early where you can, and plan a museum or rest break.
